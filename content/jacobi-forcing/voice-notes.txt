Keep
A practitioner explaining a mechanism alongside the reader. Start from a
concrete constraint and show what changes. Use plain verbs, contractions and
occasional "we" in an example. Use "I" for an opinion or documented experience.
Explain unfamiliar terms where they become useful. Connect technical choices
to waiting time, cost, implementation or answer quality. Be candid about limits.
A little dry humor can work, but it must arise from the subject.

Improve
Split sentences where their job changes from claim to mechanism to consequence.
Keep fuller explanatory sentences beside shorter observations; avoid a stream
of slogan-like fragments. Cut repeated definitions, repeated transitions,
concluding recaps and generic enthusiasm. Do not imitate old typos or comma
splices. Do not insert personality through invented experiences or forced jokes.
